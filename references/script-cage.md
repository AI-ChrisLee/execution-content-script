the asks sit), plus the public methods of the 4 most-cited script practitioners.

Practitioners: Blackman (hook = target, change, stakes; body = 5 to 7 setup, tension, payoff loops;
write the payoffs first and the hook last), Film Booth and Ed Lawrence (packaging first; the ending loops
back to the open), the MrBeast handbook (a re-hook every minute; never signal the ending; stop abruptly),
Galloway (the idea and the packaging outrank structure).

## What all 6 share

1. **HOOK, 15 to 60 seconds.** The claim with a specific number, then proof on screen before any setup
   (the finished thing, the dashboard), then what the video does, said in 1 breath and never shown as a
   list, then the proof line right after the promise, then 1 or 2 open loops, then a hard cut into the
   content.
2. **THE CASE, 0 to 25%, optional.** Never a long pain block. The winners skip it, fold the pain into the
   hook as a scene, or spend it killing the 1 objection. Short, positive, ends on a turn.
3. **THE RUBRIC.** A checklist stated early, graded again on camera halfway ("4 boxes checked, 4 to go")
   and closed at the end. The scorecard is the progress bar.
4. **THE MAIN PART, 53 to 99%.** Never 1 long arc. Numbered sections, each ending on something visible,
   each with its own small payoff 60 to 90 seconds apart. Inside it: 1 line flagging the most important
   moment, 1 real moment the system pushes back, every wait cut, and the mid ask right after the first big
   reveal, said as a resource.
5. **CLOSE, 0.3 to 17%, short and abrupt.** The rubric graded, a fresh bookend proof (a new screenshot,
   the total-cost math), the payoff against the hook's promise, 1 soft ask at most, and a pull into the
   next video. Never signal the ending early, never trail off.

Asks across the set: resource links tied to the exact step on screen read as service. Channel asks are
at most 1, at the close. The 2 biggest videos here have 0 or 1.

## The cage this agent writes

6 beats, in this order. The cage owns the order and the shares. The winner supplies the substance.

| Beat | Job | Share |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | The MAIN title said as the first sentence, the claim and its number. Then what the video does, in 1 breath. The finished result on screen inside 15 seconds | ~30s |
| PROOF | 1 line, 1 breath: why listen. Every number in it has a receipt | ~10s |
| PROBLEM | The 1 problem this video kills, in the viewer's own words, off the winner's comments. Short, ends on a turn into the solution | 5 to 10% |
| SOLUTION | The idea in the founder's own words, and the short list the video has to be true against by the end (the rubric). No commands | 5 to 10% |
| WORKFLOW | The run. Each section opens on a real question from the comments, or on a setup line, and ends on something visible, a payoff every 60 to 90 seconds. Inside: 1 line flagging the most important moment, the real moment from the prep, every wait cut, the rubric graded halfway, the 1 link right after the first big result | 60 to 75% |
| CLOSE | The rubric graded, what it costs, the payoff against the hook's promise, the 1 link, the next video in 1 line. Abrupt | 5 to 10% |

Real questions from the comments open workflow sections, where the viewer is already asking them. There
is no questions section of its own.
